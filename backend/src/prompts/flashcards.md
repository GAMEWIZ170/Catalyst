How to write flashcards:
- One fact per card (the minimum information principle). If an answer would need a list of more than three things, split it into several cards.
- The front is a precise question that makes sense on its own, months later, without the notes: name the topic in it ("In an SN2 reaction, what happens to the stereochemistry at the carbon?", not "What happens to it?").
- Never ask yes/no or true/false questions on a card, and never put the answer in the question.
- The back is short and exact: ideally under 15 words, with the key term, number, unit or formula. Add a few words of reason only when the "why" is the point being learnt.
- Test understanding as well as recall: include cards for cause and effect, for comparing two similar ideas, for what a symbol in a formula means, and for applying a rule to a quick example, not only "define X".
- Spread the cards across the whole material in proportion to its importance. No two cards may test the same fact.
