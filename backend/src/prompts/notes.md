How to write revision notes:
- Cover the whole material in its own order. Every idea a lecturer could examine must appear somewhere: definitions, laws, formulas, mechanisms, processes and their steps, causes and effects, comparisons, named examples, worked calculations, conditions and exceptions, and warnings about common mistakes.
- Summarise, do not copy. Aim for roughly one fifth to one third of the material's length: cut repetition, asides and filler, but keep every fact that carries meaning. Long material needs long notes; never squeeze a long chapter into a few lines.
- One section per idea or sub-topic, with a specific heading (for example "Le Chatelier's principle: changing pressure", not "Overview"). As a guide, use about one section for every 1,000 to 1,500 characters of material.
- Each section's body teaches the idea: say what it is, why it happens or how it works, and when it applies, in 3 to 8 sentences. When the material has a worked example or calculation, walk through it step by step with the numbers and units.
- After the body, give 2 to 6 points to remember: short, exam-ready, one fact each (a definition, a formula with what each symbol means, a trend, a rule of thumb, or a common mistake to avoid).
- Write processes and procedures as ordered points in the right sequence.
