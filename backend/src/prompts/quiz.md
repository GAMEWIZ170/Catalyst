How to write multiple choice quiz questions:
- Write exam-grade questions. Most should test applying, analysing or evaluating (Bloom's levels): solve a short problem, predict an outcome, choose the best explanation, spot the error in a claim, or compare two cases. Use plain recall for at most one question in four.
- The question text is self-contained: give every value, condition and unit needed, and ask one clear thing.
- Exactly one option is correct, and an expert would agree it is the best answer.
- Each wrong option comes from a specific misconception or a typical slip a student really makes (a sign error, a confused term, a reversed trend, a step skipped). It must look reasonable to a student who has that misconception.
- Options are similar in length, style and grammar, so the right one does not stand out. Never use "all of the above" or "none of the above". Put the correct option in different positions across the questions.
- Before you write a question, solve it yourself step by step and check that your answer matches the option you mark correct. Drop any question you are not sure of.
- The explanation says why the correct option is right (with the working for a calculation) and names the misconception behind the most tempting wrong option, in 2 to 4 sentences.

Fill in the blank questions:
- One sentence from the material's key ideas with exactly one blank written as "____", where the missing word or short phrase is a key term, value or name a student must recall.
- The blank must have one clear answer. List every acceptable form of it in "answers" (synonyms, singular and plural, a symbol and its name, with and without units), lower or mixed case does not matter.

Short answer questions:
- Ask the student to explain, justify, compare or predict in 1 to 3 sentences ("Explain why...", "What happens to ... and why?"). Never a one-word recall question.
- Give a "modelAnswer" (1 to 3 sentences, what a top student would write) and 1 to 4 "keyPoints": the separate ideas a right answer must contain, each short and checkable.
